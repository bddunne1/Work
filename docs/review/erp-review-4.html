01

Do these first

  1. Close the order-save loopholes. Stop the save from rewriting shipment history (R4-01), and take Import and Labels off the order-edit list (R4-07). Both are small changes that close most of the risk behind R4-02 and R4-03. Follow up by deciding on the server who may change each field.
  2. Cap Confirm Shipped at what was packed (R4-04). Today it can hand stock reserved for other orders to this one and take on-hand negative.
  3. Stop the double-return (R4-05) and block deleting customers who have orders (R4-06). Both are small fixes that prevent permanent damage.
  4. Make the review queues oldest-first, add Skip, and split analyst duties (R4-19, P-B2 stop-gap, P-D4). No database change, and it removes most analyst collisions today.
  5. Let analysts print what they release, and collate printing (P-A3, P-D1). One permission and a few lines of code remove a whole handoff.
  6. Fix the entry forms: blank lines, Enter saving early, duplicate POs/RAs from double-clicks, whole-number quantities (R4-14 to R4-16, R4-35).
  7. Build the end-of-day shipped report and include partial shipments (R4-20, P-A7).
  8. Fast performance wins: open POs only on Receiving and Back Orders, an index the open-orders query can use, compression, a cap on the capacity lookback, and don't auto-run reports (PF-01, 03, 04, 07, 13).
  9. Automate allocation for mechanical cases, then validate by exception (P-A2, P-A1). By the simulation's model this cuts analyst load from 133% to about 38%. Time 20 real orders per step first to confirm.
  10. Fill back orders automatically on receipt, and move the packing slip to confirm time (P-A5, P-A4). Then the dock tablet (P-B3).
02

Bugs and vulnerabilities

Each item was found by one review pass and checked against the code before it went in. Where two passes found the same problem, it's listed once and 'Found by' shows both. Known items from the bug log (H-07, M-10 … N-06) are only mentioned where this round found something new about them. Severity is for this setup: an internal network with about 15 users.

The common thread: the round-2 report said a role can't do more through the API than its screens allow. That holds for status changes, but not for the ordinary order save. A save that keeps the same status can still rewrite shipment records, allocation, the staged pick, prices and the 'checked by' stamp, and one Purchasing permission (Import) grants that save. The long-term fix is the one ship, cancel and release already got: each workflow step gets its own endpoint, and the general save only edits header fields.
R4-01Any order-page editor can delete or invent shipment records with an ordinary order saveSecurity · effort SHigh
Where
server/src/routes/salesOrders.ts:125, :446-449
What happens
The order save (PUT) accepts shipmentHistory from the browser and syncs it: rows left out are deleted, new rows are created, and no stock moves. A partly shipped order saved with shipmentHistory: [] loses its shipment records while the stock stays taken. It then gets allocated and shipped again, which takes the stock a second time. Its RAs are refused, Undo says there's nothing to undo, and it drops out of every shipped report. Adding a made-up record works the other way and closes an order without moving stock. The current screens always send the full history back, so today this needs a hand-made request or a client bug.
Fix
Ignore shipmentHistory on PUT. Only /ship and /undo-shipment should write shipment records.

R4-02A save that keeps the same status skips the step permissions for allocation, release and the 'checked by' stampSecurity · effort MHigh
Where
server/src/routes/salesOrders.ts:176-213, :404-419
What happens
The per-step permission check only runs when the status changes (if (from === to) return). On any other save the browser's allocation, pendingShipment, checkedBy/checkedAt and writtenById are written as sent. Anyone with edit on any order page can do all of this:
  • Order Entry, Logistics or Purchasing (through Import, R4-07) can raise an Allocated order's allocation to the full quantity.
  • They can raise a released order's staged pick above what was allocated.
  • They can stamp any name as the validator.
  • They can re-point who wrote the order.
Fix
Decide on the server who owns each field. Only allocation, back-orders or release editors may change allocation. Only release editors may change pendingShipment, capped at what's allocated. checkedBy/checkedAt and writtenBy should come from the signed-in account, never from the browser. Longer term, give each step its own endpoint, as ship, cancel and release already have.

R4-04Confirm Shipped takes any quantity and can use stock reserved for other ordersData integrity · effort SHigh
Where
server/src/routes/salesOrders.ts:518-529; src/pages/OpenPicksDetail.tsx:225-228, :258
What happens
The /ship endpoint only checks that the line belongs to the order. It never compares the quantity with what was packed (pendingShipment) or with free stock. SEC notes
OKChecked and found soundWhat the passes looked at and didn't flag
  • Sign-in: tokens need a real secret with no fallback. Signing someone out, resetting a password or deactivating an account takes effect on their very next request, and permissions are re-read each time. Passwords are hashed with bcrypt and sign-in attempts are throttled. Only admins can change accounts, and the last admin is protected.
  • Database queries: no SQL injection. All raw SQL is parameterized and search sorting comes from a fixed list.
  • Stock-moving actions: ship, undo, cancel, bulk release, PO receive and RA receive each run as one locked transaction with a version check, and always write a stock-ledger row.
  • Browser: no dangerouslySetInnerHTML, innerHTML or eval anywhere. CSV exports neutralize formula cells. Errors never send stack traces.
  • Release Orders and Dashboard (rebuilt in round 3): they keep the server's copy after saves, lock buttons while busy, use local dates, and hide actions the server would refuse.
  • Other pages: every link goes to a real route. Every confirm dialog stops on Cancel.
03

Inefficiencies

Growth figures come from the round-2 one-year scale test (37,800 orders; loading every order took 163 MB and 14 s) and from live measurements on the test database: 4.9 KB per open order, 2.6 KB per PO, and gzip shrinking data 5.7–6.8×. The test database is small (93 orders), so today's timings are fast. These items are about what grows.

Where
src/lib/reports/dataSources.ts:97-101, :54-78; src/pages/ReportRunner.tsx:63-75
What happens
With no catalog match, the item filter is dropped and the report fetches every order in 500-order pages: about 163 MB after one year and about 490 MB after three, only to show nothing. Choosing 'Sales Orders' also runs straight away with no filters and downloads about 21 MB (5,000 full orders).
Fix
Return 'no rows' when the item matches nothing. Don't auto-run, and require a date range for order sources. Longer term, have the server return report rows directly.

Side paths

  • Back order: the queue shows projected dates but not 'can fill now'. Each order is opened to find out, then steps 3–9 run again. Nothing happens when a PO is received.
  • Partial ship: steps 3–9 repeat for each shipment.
  • Cancel: Customer Service or an analyst can cancel from any status except Shipped. A printed pick vanishes from the screens but stays on the floor (R4-23).
  • Customer change after allocation: lines are locked. Customer Service can't unallocate, so it becomes Customer Service → analyst → Customer Service → analyst. A back-ordered quantity can't be revised at all (R4-18).
  • Returns: the RA is re-keyed line by line from free text. The dock receives it and restocks. No credit memo follows.

Supporting flows

  • Buying: no page lists items below their reorder point or short against demand. The buyer works it out by hand (15 min per PO run in the simulation).

  • Price and availability quotes (Customer Service): Customer Pricing and Inventory are two separate pages. 'Available' ignores orders that aren't allocated yet, and about 100 orders sat unvalidated all day in the simulation.
  • End of day (Logistics): there's no screen for it (R4-20).

Share of two analysts' 960 minutes a day that 150 orders need (the black mark is 100%). Today: 8.54 min per order = 1,281 min. After: about 1.9 min per order plus about 80 min/day of back-order work. Sources: the simulation's step times and its order mix (58% fully in stock, 19% held, 24% partial).

  • W-1 · Analysts are the bottleneck, and most of their work is mechanical. Each order needs 8.5 analyst-minutes (validate 2.4, allocate 3.3, release 1.9, plus 0.9 switching between orders). At 150 orders/day that's 1,281 min against 960 available, 133%. In the simulation only 33 of 144 orders entered that day were validated, and 91 that came in before 3 pm were still unvalidated at close. After validation things move fast (checked→allocated median 7 min, allocated→released 4 min). All the waiting is in front of validation.
  • W-2 · Queues take the newest order first, have no claim, and have no Skip. The backlog starves while new orders keep arriving. Two analysts collide on the same first order: 60 refusals/day with 2 analysts, 150 with 3. A stale page is a dead end.
  • W-3 · A handoff exists only to print. The person who releases can't print, so every order waits in a second queue for Order Entry, which is already 78–89% busy.
  • W-4 · The packing slip is printed before the pick. Every short leaves the slip in the box wrong unless logistics reprints it.
  • W-5 · Paper to screen re-keying at ship confirm. Pickers write down shorts and the one logistics user types them in (71–83% busy).
  • W-6 · Re-keying on shipping and receiving documents. BOL weight, carrier and PRO; label addresses; RA lines; every PO receipt line; customer part numbers at entry.
  • W-7 · Back orders wait until someone looks. A PO receipt doesn't bring up the back orders it could fill, and the Back Order Queue can't show 'fillable now'. There were 36–50 back orders at close in the simulation.
  • W-8 · Switching between pages. Customer Service needs two pages for a price-and-availability quote. Truck planning needs five pages. A validation fix means three page changes and losing your place in the queue.
  • W-9 · A checked order can change without being checked again. See R4-22.
  • W-10 · A cancelled pick stays on the floor unseen. See R4-23.
  • W-11 · No end-of-day shipped report. A daily 10-minute workaround, or it doesn't get done (R4-20).
  • W-12 · Checking the same fact twice. Ship-complete is asked again on every short order although it's a customer setting. The header copied from the customer record is re-read at validation. 'Released' and 'printed' are two queues for one physical event.

Method and caveats
