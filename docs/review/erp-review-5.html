01

The take

This is a well-built order-management and warehouse system with purchasing and returns attached. It is not yet an ERP in the sense the name usually carries. There is no invoicing, no receivables or payables, no costing and no ledger. That is not a criticism of the work, but it is the frame for every recommendation below: the strongest parts of the codebase are exactly the parts an accounting package does not do, and the weakest parts are the seams where a whole-object save can rewrite something that should be server-owned.

Two things stand out after reading every server route. First, the atomic stock operations, the optimistic versioning and the allocation lock are genuinely good and rare in a system this size. Second, the generic PUT for orders and vendor POs undoes some of that care: it lets a caller with any of a dozen page permissions rewrite shipment history, received quantities and who checked an order, and the ship endpoint never checks quantities at all. The single highest-value change is to finish the move to command endpoints that the ship, receive, cancel and release routes already started.

On the accounting question, the short version: do not build it. Build invoicing as a first-class event in this system and push it to QuickBooks, which stays the system of record for money. Section 05 makes the case in full.

02

What holds up

Stock is atomic and attributable

Ship, undo-ship, PO receipt and return receipt each run in one transaction and write a StockMovement row with actor, reason and reference. Every on-hand figure can be explained from the ledger. This is the correct foundation and most homegrown systems never reach it.

Concurrency is handled on purpose

Whole-object saves carry a version and fail with a 409 instead of overwriting. Ship and receive take a row lock. Allocation increases serialize behind an advisory lock in server/src/lib/inventory.ts, so two analysts cannot both be told the last case is free.

The workflow is enforced server-side

The transition matrix in routes/salesOrders.ts decides which page permission may move an order between which statuses, blocks sideways moves, freezes lines once stock is committed, and routes ship, cancel and undo through dedicated endpoints. Document numbers come from one atomic counter.

The review habit

Three prior rounds with a 15-user simulated workday, a one-year scale benchmark, a running bug log and design options with mockups. The comments in the code explain why, not what. Keep doing this; it is why the findings below are mostly seams rather than rot.

Findings

Everything in docs/review/open-bugs.md still stands. These are new. Severity is rated for an internal network with about 15 trusted users; several would be rated higher if a warehouse kiosk or an outside party ever gets a login. R5-01The ship endpoint accepts any quantity on any lineOver-shipping, shipping unreleased lines and negative on-hand are all possible through the APIHigh
Where
server/src/routes/salesOrders.ts lines 505 to 548 (POST /:soNumber/ship)
Problem
The handler checks only that each lineItemId belongs to the order. It never compares the quantity to what was staged in pendingShipment, to what remains on the line, or to what is on hand. A buggy client or a crafted request can ship 500 units against a 10-unit line, mark the order Shipped, drive on-hand negative, and consume stock that other orders have reserved. The UI clamps the number, but OpenPicks.tsx ships pendingShipment as-is and the server is the only real guard.
Fix
Reject any line not in pendingShipment, cap each quantity at the staged amount, and refuse a shipment that would take on-hand below zero unless an explicit override permission is present.
R5-02Who entered and who checked an order is whatever the client saysThe accountability stamps the audit trail depends on are forgeableHigh
Where
routes/salesOrders.ts schema lines 109 to 118 and writes at 404 to 410; routes/returns.ts lines 50 to 52
Problem
writtenBy, writtenById, checkedBy, checkedAt and the colours arrive in the request body and are stored as sent. Anyone with order-write access can stamp another person's initials on a validation or re-date a check. Cancellation gets this right and stamps cancelledBy from the token; the rest should too.
Fix
Strip these fields from the input schemas. Set writer fields on create and checker fields inside a dedicated check command, both from req.account.
R5-03Shipment history and PO receipts can be rewritten by a plain saveDocuments and the stock ledger can be made to disagree without any stock movementHigh
Where
routes/salesOrders.ts line 125 and the sync at 446; routes/vendorPurchaseOrders.ts lines 37 to 45 and 184 to 195
Problem
The order PUT accepts a full shipmentHistory array and syncs it to the table, deleting rows the client omitted. The vendor PO PUT accepts per-line receivedQty, a full receivingHistory, and a status of Received or Closed. Either lets a user "receive" or "un-ship" on paper while on-hand and the ledger stay untouched. The version check prevents accidents from a stale screen but not a fresh, deliberate or buggy client.
Fix
Make these server-owned: ignore or reject them on PUT, derive PO status from line quantities, and let only the ship, undo, receive and return-receive commands change them.
R5-04Credentials and sessions are weaker than the rest of the systemDefault admin, one-character passwords, 30-day tokens in localStorage, no idle timeoutHigh
Where
server/src/seed.ts; routes/accounts.ts lines 21 and 33; middleware/auth.ts line 27; src/lib/apiClient.ts
Problem
H-07 from round 1 is still open. Beyond it: password minimum is one character, tokens live 30 days and sit in localStorage, and nothing logs a person out of a shared warehouse PC left unattended. Token revocation via tokenVersion is good, but nothing triggers it automatically.
Fix
Seed a random admin password printed once. Enforce a 10-character minimum and a forced change on first login. Move to 8-hour tokens with a sliding idle timeout in the client. Consider a short-lived access token with a refresh cookie if the app ever leaves the LAN.
R5-05Page permissions grant far more than the page doesTwelve different page keys each allow a full rewrite of an order, prices includedMedium
Where
routes/salesOrders.ts lines 176 to 189 (ORDER_WRITE_PAGES)
Problem
Edit access on Labels, BOL, Schedule or Import is enough to change an order's customer, addresses, rates and, while Entered or Checked, its quantities. The page model was built for navigation and cannot express "may set a BOL but may not change a price". This is also the root of the writer carve-out mismatch (N-03) and the internal page-key names in errors (N-04).
Fix
Command endpoints with narrow inputs (section 04, step 1). Each page then needs one permission that does exactly one thing.
R5-06The login throttle can lock any known user out and does not see real client addressesPer-username denial of service; shared IP behind a proxy; unbounded mapMedium
Where
routes/auth.ts lines 15 to 35
Problem
Ten wrong passwords for a username lock that username for 15 minutes. Anyone who knows a colleague's login can keep them out indefinitely. Express is not told to trust a proxy, so behind nginx or Caddy every client shares one address and the key degenerates to username alone. The failures map is never pruned.
Fix
Set trust proxy in production, add a slow-down instead of a hard lock, and prune expired entries. Log throttle events to the audit log so a lockout campaign is visible.
R5-07Any signed-in account can download every order with every priceRead access is far broader than the permission model suggestsMedium
Where
routes/salesOrders.ts line 230 (unfiltered list); routes/items.ts line 65; routes/customers.ts line 98 (summary mode)
Problem
The comments explain the reasoning (the Dashboard shows orders to everyone), and for 15 trusted staff it is defensible. It stops being defensible the day a warehouse kiosk, a temp or an outside contractor gets a login: the whole order book with line prices and every customer's addresses is one request away.
Fix
Serve the Dashboard from a purpose-built summary endpoint that returns counts and queue rows without prices. Gate the full list behind an order-view permission. Strip rate from list responses for accounts without a pricing permission.
R5-08The audit log can miss changes and never records what changedFire-and-forget outside the transaction; status only; settings and counters unloggedMedium
Where
server/src/lib/audit.ts; routes/settings.ts line 31; routes/counters.ts line 64; routes/savedReports.ts
Problem
Audit writes happen after the transaction commits and errors are swallowed, so a committed change can have no entry. ORDER_UPDATED records only the status, so "who changed the price on line 3" is unanswerable. Settings edits, counter overrides and report changes are not logged at all.
Fix
Write the audit row inside the same transaction. Store a before/after diff of the fields that matter (rates, quantities, addresses, dates). Log every administrative write.
R5-09Most non-conflict errors vanish without a messageClick handlers rethrow inside promises; there is no error boundaryMedium
Where
src/pages/AllocationDecision.tsx lines 167 to 174; ValidationDecision.tsx lines 65 to 70; OpenPicksDetail.tsx lines 71 to 78 and 99 to 106; src/App.tsx
Problem
The decision pages catch a 409 and alert it, then throw err for everything else. Inside an async click handler that rejection goes nowhere, so a 400 ("not in the catalog"), a 403 or a 500 shows the user nothing and the order appears to save. There is no React error boundary and no global unhandledrejection handler.
Fix
One shared handleApiError that shows a toast for every failure, an error boundary around Outlet, and a window-level rejection handler as the last line of defence.
R5-10Item numbers are unique case-sensitively but looked up case-insensitivelyTwo spellings of one item can coexist, and lookups then pick one at randomMedium
Where
server/prisma/schema.prisma line 198; server/src/lib/inventory.ts lines 10 to 14 and 68 to 87
Problem
The database allows BR-1001 and br-1001 as two items. Every lookup is case-insensitive, so findFirst returns whichever Postgres finds first and the wrong item's stock moves.
Fix
A unique index on lower(trim("itemNumber")) via a raw migration, and normalise on write.
R5-11A partly shipped order can have its shipped lines edited or removedOnce unallocated back to Checked, the line freeze no longer appliesMedium
Where
routes/salesOrders.ts lines 366 to 383 and the transition matrix at 203
Problem
Lines are frozen only while status is outside Entered and Checked. A partly shipped order goes Backordered, then Allocated, then can be unallocated to Checked. At that point a line with shipment history can be deleted or its quantity cut below what shipped. The shipment record's JSON then points at a missing line, undo-shipment silently skips it, and the order can never reconcile.
Fix
Freeze any line that appears in shipmentHistory regardless of status, and never let ordered drop below the shipped total.
R5-12"How much is free?" is answered by loading every open orderReservations live in JSON on the order, so both client and server scan the whole open bookMedium
Where
server/src/lib/inventory.ts lines 160 to 167; src/pages/AllocationDecision.tsx line 39; src/lib/reports/dataSources.ts inventory source
Problem
allocation and pendingShipment are JSON blobs keyed by line id. To compute available stock the server fetches every open order with lines under the advisory lock, and every allocation page downloads the same set to redo the math in the browser. Fine at 500 open orders; a serialisation bottleneck at 5,000. It also means the client re-implements reservation rules that should exist once.
Fix

04

Redesign plan, in priority order

Six moves. The first one closes most of the high findings on its own, and the second removes the last place where the client re-implements server rules. Each step names the findings it closes so the bug log can be updated as work lands.

  1. Replace the whole-object order PUT with command endpoints

    The ship, undo, cancel, receive and release routes already show the pattern. Extend it: check, allocate, unallocate, set-ship-date, stamp-printed, set-bol, and an edit that accepts header fields and lines only while the order is Entered or Checked. Each command has a narrow zod schema, stamps the actor from the token, and is gated by exactly one permission. Status, attribution, shipment history and received quantities become server-owned and are rejected if sent. Do the same for vendor POs.

    • Ship validates every line against pendingShipment and on-hand.
    • Pages send one small payload instead of the whole record, which ends the 5 MB customer problem's cousins on orders.
    • Permission names in errors come from one shared page map used by client and server.
    Closes R5-01, R5-02, R5-03, R5-05, R5-11 and open bugs N-03, N-04, part of L-06.
  2. Normalise reservations

    Move allocation and pendingShipment out of JSON into an Allocation table with soNumber, lineId, itemId, allocatedQty, stagedQty. Maintain Item.qtyReserved in the same transactions that write it, exactly as qtyOnHand is maintained today. Available stock becomes one indexed read, the advisory lock protects a single row update instead of a full scan, and the Dashboard, allocation page and inventory report stop downloading the open order book.

    Closes R5-12 and open bugs N-05, N-06, M-12b, M-13's first half.
  3. Tests and CI before more features

    Vitest against a throwaway Postgres (Testcontainers or a CI service) for inventory.ts and the order commands, with explicit tests for: ship twice with a stale version, two allocations racing for the last unit, undo after a repack, receive against a closed PO, and rename an item with history. A Playwright smoke run over the five core flows in the simulation. GitHub Actions running typecheck, oxlint, tests and prisma migrate diff on every push to a branch.

    Closes R5-19. Makes steps 1 and 2 safe to do.
  4. Deployment and session hardening

    Serve the built frontend from the API (or behind Caddy) on one origin with TLS, drop CORS, add helmet, set trust proxy. Eight-hour tokens with a client idle timeout. Ten-character passwords, forced change on first login, random seeded admin password. Slow-down instead of hard lockout. A deployment and backup runbook in the README.

    Closes R5-04, R5-06, R5-21, R5-20 and open bugs H-07, L-03.
  5. Schema pass

    One migration: updatedAt and updatedBy everywhere, Item.unitCost with a cost snapshot on shipped lines, a case-insensitive unique index on item number, the four date indexes, a diff column on audit rows written inside the transaction, non-negative money constraints, and the quantity-precision decision from R5-15. Move money math on the client to integer cents.

    Closes R5-08, R5-10, R5-13, R5-14, R5-15, R5-16, R5-18 and open bugs L-04, M-15, L-02.
  6. Frontend plumbing

    TanStack Query for fetching, caching and invalidation, so pages stop re-downloading the catalog. A toast component to replace the 34 alert() calls, an error boundary around the router outlet and a global rejection handler. Split GenerateBOL.tsx (975 lines) and PickPack.tsx (735 lines) into form and print views. Move PAGE_DEFS into a shared package imported by both client and server.

    Closes R5-09, R5-17 and open bugs L-06, N-02, M-10, M-11.
05

The accounting question

Would it be easier to grow this into a real ERP with invoicing and accounting, or leave those to QuickBooks? The honest answer has two parts, because "invoicing" and "accounting" are different sizes of problem.

Invoicing is small and belongs here. An invoice is a document generated from a shipment, with the prices and tax this system already knows. The data model is nearly ready: ShipmentRecord is already one row per shipment, rates are stored as decimals, and returns are already a separate document. Owning the invoice means the customer gets a correct invoice the moment the truck leaves, with your part numbers, their PO number and your pricing logic, and nobody re-keys anything.

Accounting is large and does not belong here. Being the book of record means receivables with payment application, partial payments, credit memos and aging; payables with vendor bills and payment runs; a double-entry ledger with a chart of accounts, journal entries and period close; sales tax by jurisdiction with filing; bank feeds and reconciliation; financial statements; 1099s; year-end; and your accountant's access and habits. None of it is technically exotic. All of it is unforgiving, changes with tax law, and has to be right every single month with no "reload and try again". Payroll is a separate universe nobody builds.

What to build for Option B

  • Invoice model. Invoice with number, soNumber, shipmentRecordId, line snapshots (item, description, qty, rate, extended), tax, terms, due date, status (Draft, Issued, Synced, Void). Generated automatically on ship; printable like the BOL.
  • Credit memo model. Same shape, created on return receipt, priced from the original invoice line, which fixes M-15's third point.
  • Mapping tables. ExternalRef(entityType, entityId, system, externalId) for customers, items, invoices and credit memos. QuickBooks ids are the join key; never rely on names.
  • Sync worker. An outbox table written in the same transaction as the invoice, a worker that pushes in order with retry and backoff, and a SyncLog the Activity Log page can show. Idempotent by invoice number.
  • Reconciliation report. Invoices issued here versus invoices in QuickBooks for a date range, with the differences. Run it daily for the first quarter.
  • Prerequisites from section 04. Integer-cent money math (L-04), updatedAt for change detection (R5-13), and non-negative prices (R5-18). A tax code per customer rather than a bare rate per order, because QuickBooks wants a tax code.

Decisions that change the estimate

  • QuickBooks Online or Desktop? Online has a REST API with OAuth 2, a sandbox company and good documentation; the integration is a few weeks. Desktop needs the Web Connector and QBXML, or a paid connector, or an IIF file import. If you are on Desktop, start with a nightly IIF export (Option C) and decide whether moving to Online is worth it for this alone.
  • Who is the customer of record? If QuickBooks already has customers, do a one-time match on account number and store the ids; make this app authoritative from then on.
  • Invoice per shipment or per order? Per shipment matches the ledger and how partials actually bill. Confirm with whoever does collections.
  • Items in QuickBooks. Create them as non-inventory or service items so QuickBooks never tracks quantity. If they are already inventory items, plan the conversion.
  • Sales tax. If most customers are exempt resellers, a per-customer tax code is enough. If you collect in several states, let QuickBooks or its tax service compute it and store the result back on the invoice.

When would building accounting be the right call? When you outgrow QuickBooks: multiple legal entities to consolidate, revenue recognition rules, or an auditor who wants controls QuickBooks cannot give. At that point the answer is still not to build; it is to move to a mid-market package and keep this system as the operations layer in front of it. The integration boundary described above is the same one you would need then, which is another reason to draw it now.

One more honest note. The instinct to keep everything in one system is sound, and the code quality here suggests you could build a passable AR module. The trouble is not building it; it is being on the hook for it at month-end, at tax time and at audit, forever, while also being the person who ships rope. QuickBooks costs a subscription. Owning the ledger costs your attention.

Scope and method

This round is a static review: every file under server/src, the Prisma schema and migrations, the frontend stores and permission layer, the workflow decision pages, the report data sources, the simulation harness and the three earlier review documents. Nothing was executed against a database this round, so line references are exact but no timing or load figures are new; the round 2 and 3 numbers still apply.

Findings are numbered R5-01 to R5-21 and should be added to docs/review/open-bugs.md alongside the H, M, L and N series. Where a new finding overlaps an open one, the finding text says which.
