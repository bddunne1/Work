01

The short version

The system started the week as a sound workflow design on a fresh Postgres backend that, as configured, could not ship or receive, corrupted stock when two people worked at once, and stopped responding after a year of orders. Round 1 fixed the corruption by moving every stock change into the transaction that causes it. Round 2 added the seven roles, paging, cancellation, restock, item links, the stock ledger and a reliable import, and a simulated day ended with the books exact. Round 3 rebuilt Release Orders and the Dashboard around how people actually queue work. Round 4 read the whole codebase again and found that the ordinary order save was still a loophole around the step permissions, then mapped where analyst time goes. Round 5 closed the loophole by making every workflow step its own command, hardened sign-in, added a test suite that races the transactions and audits the invariants after a random concurrent workload, and built invoicing with a bridge to QuickBooks.

Sprint 1 (29 September) closed the integrity items and the quick wins: 24 items closed and 3 partly done, with 15 new tests, a Postgres deadlock the workload test found and fixed, and the four decisions of 29 September applied (price lock, undo after sync, Import creates only, customer required on Order Entry). What is left falls into three groups. A middle tier of medium screen and performance items, mostly quick, listed in section 04 with the exact next step for each. The throughput redesign from rounds 2 and 4, which is the only thing on this list that changes what the business can do in a day and which has had no code written against it. And a handful of decisions only you can make, in section 07.

Recommended order from here: merge and deploy the sprint 1 branch, then sprint 2 (the reservation table, read exposure, audit in the transaction and the remaining medium items), then sprint 3 for the throughput work with the no-code changes and auto-allocation. The sprint plan (erp-sprint-plan.html) has each sprint as tasks. Measure real step times before committing to rules-based validation.

The two things to do before anything else are done. A-13 (returns counted twice) and A-14 (customer delete) closed in sprint 1 with tests, along with undo after a received return (A-15) and the price lock (B-06). Section 06 still applies to putting the build into use.

03

Where the system stands

What a new engineer should know is true of the codebase on the branch today, and the numbers that back it.

Stock is a ledger, not a number

Every change to on-hand is a StockMovement written in the same transaction as the document that caused it, with the actor. Ship, undo, receive and return-receive lock the row, check the version and refuse to leave on-hand negative. The randomized workload test asserts start plus movements equals on-hand equals documents for every item after eight workers hammer the API.

Every step is a command

check, uncheck, allocate, unallocate, release, mark-printed, set-ship-date, set-bol, ship, undo-shipment, cancel. Each is gated by one page permission and stamps who did it from the session. The plain save edits header fields and, while Entered or Checked, lines. A permission-matrix test proves each role can do exactly its own step.

Money documents exist

An invoice per shipment and a credit memo per received return, in integer cents, priced from the order and the invoice respectively. An outbox row is written with each and a worker pushes it to QuickBooks with backoff, creating customers and non-inventory items first. QuickBooks remains the record for receivables, payments, tax and the ledger.

Roles

Seven presets: Purchasing, Customer Service, Order Entry, Analyst, Logistics, Sales Manager, Director, plus Admin. Round 5 added an Invoices page key (Customer Service and Sales Manager view, Director edit). Analysts still cannot print what they release (G-02).

Sign-in

Ten-character passwords with a deny list; a seeded or admin-set password must be replaced at first sign-in; ten-hour tokens; 45-minute idle logout; force-logout and deactivation take effect on the next request; throttled and constant-time sign-in. Still to deploy: one origin behind TLS.

Scale

Order history is paged everywhere except the capacity banner and the report edge case in D-02 and D-05. At a year of data every page opened for 15 people in under a second except Release Orders at 6.4 s. The reservation model still scans open orders (A-25); fine at hundreds, a bottleneck at thousands.

What the simulations measured and what they assumed. Stock and order integrity results do not depend on hardware or timing assumptions. The throughput results do: they assume validation takes about 1 minute plus 9 seconds per line, allocation 1 minute plus 15 seconds per line, release 1 minute plus 6 seconds per line, and a floor time of 10 minutes plus 1.5 per line. Time twenty real orders per step before sizing staff or committing to the larger automation.

06

Deploying the current branch

Round 2's four steps, extended for what round 5 changed. Do it once against a copy of production first.

  1. Back up. pg_dump -Fc erp_prod > before-round5.dump. Both new migrations are additive, but one adds a unique index that fails if two item numbers differ only by case; resolve those under Items first, or the migration will tell you which.
  2. Merge by pull request from claude/sweet-mccarthy-iug4f3 into main. CI runs the whole suite against a fresh Postgres. The branch already includes main's round 4 report.
  3. Environment. In server/.env keep DATABASE_URL and JWT_SECRET; add TOKEN_TTL=10h, TRUST_PROXY=1 if behind a proxy, CORS_ORIGIN for the web origin, TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY from openssl rand -hex 32, and the five QBO_* variables once the Intuit app exists. QBO_SYNC_INTERVAL_MS=0 disables the worker until you are ready.
  4. Migrate and restart. cd server && npx prisma migrate deploy, then restart the API. The frontend needs VITE_API_URL at build time as before; VITE_IDLE_MINUTES overrides the 45-minute idle logout.
  5. Accounts. Existing passwords keep working. Reset the admin password from Accounts (the person is then asked to choose their own), or run the seed on an empty database to get a random one. Re-apply a preset to Customer Service, Sales Manager and Director accounts so they gain the Invoices page. Create a second admin (B-17).
  6. Behaviour your team will notice. Ten-character passwords and a forced change after a reset; sign-out after 45 idle minutes; an invoice appears for every shipment and a credit memo for every received return; the RA and PO forms no longer accept received quantities typed by hand; Release Orders and Open Picks refuse to ship more than was staged.
  7. QuickBooks. Connect the sandbox company first from Settings, ship a test order, run Sync now, check the invoice in QuickBooks, then reconcile the date range. Turn off inventory tracking for the items QuickBooks creates. Only then connect production.
  8. Verify. Sign in as each role and walk one order end to end; open the Activity Log and confirm ORDER_SHIPPED carries the invoice number; run sim/day.mjs against a copy if you have the time.
07

Decisions only you can make

Accounting and tax

  • QuickBooks Online or Desktop? The bridge is built for Online. Desktop needs the Web Connector or a file export instead (round 5, section 05).
  • Who owns the tax figure once Automated Sales Tax is on: send our TotalTax, or send lines and let QuickBooks compute (E-09)? Decide after reconciling the first month.
  • Invoice per shipment is what is built. Confirm with whoever does collections that partial shipments should bill separately.
  • Undo after invoicing. Undoing a shipment voids its invoice. Should that be refused once the invoice has synced, or once it is paid (A-15)?

Product and policy

  • Is anything sold by the foot? Decides decimal quantities and a units table (E-06).
  • Allocating ahead of a delivery. Since round 1 allocation refuses to reserve more than is free. If you do this on purpose, an override is needed.
  • Price lock point. Once released, once shipped, or never for Customer Service (B-06)?
  • Should Import edit existing orders or only create (B-07)?
  • Rename the status Pick & Packed to Released (C-17)?

Staffing and process

  • Time twenty real orders at validation, allocation and release. Every throughput number here depends on it.
  • Third analyst, or Customer Service validates phone orders, or skip validation for small repeat orders from key accounts (G-12)?
  • Split analyst duties now (one validates, one allocates and releases)? No code needed (G-02).
  • A dock tablet for confirming picks, which needs a Warehouse role (G-09)?

Operations

  • Where does it run? One origin behind TLS is the recommendation (B-15); who administers the host and the backups (F-02)?
  • Read exposure. Is a warehouse kiosk or an outside login likely? If so, B-10 moves up.
  • Second admin account holder (B-17).
