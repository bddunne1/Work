02 · Roles

Seven roles, enforced by the server

Each role is a preset on the Accounts page. The server checks the same permissions, so a role can't do more through the API than its screens allow. Status changes are checked step by step:

  • Entered → Checked needs Validation.
  • Checked → Allocated needs Allocation.
  • Allocated → Pick & Packed needs the new Release Picks permission.
  • Confirming a shipment needs Open Picks.
  • Cancelling needs Sales Order View or Allocation.
  • Nobody can skip a step.

Pick & Pack is split in two, so Order Entry can print released picks without releasing them.

Roles you may have missed

  • Warehouse floor (pick and pack). Nobody in the roster physically picks. The sim models the floor as time between printing a pick and logistics confirming it, and logistics keys in any shorts. A Warehouse role on a tablet at the dock could confirm picks and record shorts directly, which would take load off the single logistics person (71% busy).
  • Accounting / billing. Nothing invoices shipped orders, applies payments or turns returns into credit memos. You need either this role and module, or an export to your accounting system.
  • System admin. Right now only the Director has the Admin role, which covers account management. A second admin avoids a single point of failure for password resets and new hires.
  • Sales managers' lead capture. The app has no leads, quotes or follow-ups. Sales managers can only add a customer once it's won. This gap is on the to-do list.
03 · Simulation

A day with the new team

The 15 people above worked one 8-hour day, compressed into 12 minutes, against the real API. The load was the same as round 1: 150 new orders plus 60 left over from yesterday, the top 20 customers placing 80% of them, and 10% of orders with 20 or more lines. How each role spent the day:

  • Order entry keyed 55% of the orders and printed picks.
  • Customer service took the phone orders, plus status calls, quotes, returns and cancellations.
  • Analysts validated, allocated and released picks.
  • The warehouse floor took 10 minutes plus about 1.5 minutes per line to pick an order after printing.
  • Logistics confirmed shipments, booked pickups, generated BOLs and ran the end-of-day report.
  • Purchasing received trucks and returns, wrote POs and ran cycle counts.
  • Sales managers opened new accounts and worked their existing ones.

Busy time is what each person spent on tasks. Idle time is waiting for work to show up.

Staffing: each order takes an analyst about 8 minutes across validation, allocation and pick release, plus page loads, re-checking back orders and collisions. At 150 orders a day that's more than two people can do; three nearly keep up at steady state, but not with yesterday's backlog on top. The options, cheapest first:

  • Let customer service validate the phone orders they take. That's a one-line change to their preset, and they run at about 55% busy.
  • Skip separate validation for small repeat orders from key accounts.
  • Add a third analyst (a fourth to clear backlogs).
  • Time real reviews first: if your analysts are faster than my assumptions, the gap shrinks.

What-if, one customer service rep moved to Analyst (3 CS, 3 analysts): the orders that arrived before 3 pm and were still waiting for validation fell from 91 to 70. Units shipped rose from 46,209 to 62,716 (+36%). The three analysts were still 99% busy, so three is better but not enough at these time assumptions. Customer service still kept up with every phone order at 55% busy. Collisions at the top of the shared queue rose from 60 to 150 refusals; splitting the queues (one person validates, the others allocate and release) would avoid most of them.

These conclusions depend on my time assumptions for each task. Validating takes 1 minute plus 9 seconds per line, allocating 1 minute plus 15 seconds per line, and releasing 1 minute plus 6 seconds per line. Time a few real orders; if your analysts are faster, the gap shrinks. Other options besides hiring: let customer service validate the phone orders they took (a one-line preset change), or skip separate validation for small repeat orders from key accounts.

05 · Built

What changed this round

Step 2 · Paging

A paged order search (status, customer, item, PO, and order and ship dates) and server-side analytics replace the full-history downloads on these pages:

  • Open and Closed Orders
  • Shipment History
  • Analytics
  • Reports (capped at 5,000 rows, with a notice)
  • Item Profile
  • Item Quick Report (which now matches the exact item)

Order Entry, Pricing and Returns load a slim customer list and fetch one customer's price sheet when it's picked. Schedule Shipments, Product Labels and the Settings number check no longer load history either.

Step 4 · Cancel and restock

  • Cancel Order needs a reason, releases allocated and packed stock, and takes the order out of every queue.
  • Receive Return puts each line marked restock back on hand, and logs damaged lines as scrap.
  • An RA tied to an S.O. can't return more than that order shipped.
  • An RA can no longer be marked Received by a plain save.

Step 5 · Item links

  • Order, PO and return lines carry the catalog item's id, and existing lines were backfilled.
  • Unknown items are rejected when an order is saved, with the item named.
  • Renaming an item carries its orders, POs, returns, customer prices and part numbers with it.
  • An item with history can't be deleted.

Step 6 · Audit trail

  • Every change to on-hand (ship, undo, receive, return, count, edit) writes a stock-ledger row in the same transaction, with who did it and which document caused it.
  • The Activity Log records order, PO, return, item, customer and vendor events, with filters and a Stock ledger tab.
  • Item Profile shows the item's stock history.

Step 7 · Import

  • Rows are saved one at a time; a failure never stops the run.
  • Each row gets a result, and failed rows can be downloaded as a CSV.
  • Existing records and duplicates within the file are skipped.
  • Amounts like "$1,234.50" parse; anything else is a row error.
  • The button is locked while saving, and view-only accounts can't import.

Roles and workflow rules

  • Seven presets.
  • A separate Release Picks permission.
  • Each status change is checked, with no skipping steps.
  • An order's lines are frozen once stock is committed (unallocate first to change them).
  • Stale screens are refused.

07 · Main

Pushing to main for tonight's tests

Recommendation: merge through a pull request tonight, after these four steps:

  1. Back up the database (pg_dump -Fc). The new migration is additive: a new status value, new columns and a new stock-ledger table, with existing lines linked to items automatically. Still, keep a backup.
  2. Apply the migration with cd server && npx prisma migrate deploy, then restart the API.
  3. Re-apply a role preset to each account on the Accounts page. Existing accounts keep their old permissions, and anyone who releases picks now needs the new Release Picks permission.
  4. Change the admin password.

Behavior your team will notice:

  • Allocation won't reserve more than is free.
  • Orders with items that aren't in the catalog are refused.
  • Allocated orders can't have their lines edited until they're unallocated.
  • Only logistics (Open Picks / Shipment History) can confirm or undo shipments.
  • An RA is received with the Receive Return button.
