Aamstrand ERP Review

The workflow design is sound and the move to Postgres is nearly done. As the app is set up today, though, the warehouse can't ship and the dock can't receive. Once that's worked around, simultaneous users corrupt on-hand stock. And the app stops responding once it holds about a year of orders. All three are fixed on the review branch; the details are below.

01 · Summary

Where it stands

Today, as configured 0 units

shipped by 15 staff in a full simulated day. All 66 ship attempts and all 43 receiving attempts were refused because the Warehouse role lacks a permission the server requires.

Today, with the permission workaround 16,250 units

of on-hand stock recorded wrong across 66 SKUs after one day. 15 SKUs went negative, 916 units were promised to two orders at once, and 18 orders were pushed back to an earlier stage.

After the fixes 0 errors

in stock, allocation or order status after the same day. 70 orders and 76,365 units shipped. Every collision between users was refused with a clear message instead of corrupting data.

The biggest risk is how stock moves. When an order ships or a PO is received, the browser changes each item's quantity one request at a time and only then saves the order. If anyone else touched that order in the meantime, the save is rejected, but the stock has already moved. The screen says "reload and try again", and trying again moves it a second time. With 15 people working the same queues this happens every day. The fixes move ship, receive and undo into single database transactions, check free stock on the server when allocating, and stop a stale screen from pushing an order back to an earlier stage.

Found 34 issues: 3 critical, 10 high, 15 medium, 6 low. 19 are fixed, 5 are partly fixed and 10 are open, each with a recommendation. The fixes are in two commits on the review branch, and nothing was merged. The frontend and API both typecheck and lint clean, and a browser run confirmed each role's pages and the ship and receive flows (46 of 46 checks passed).

02 · Simulation

15 people, one working day

Fifteen scripted staff worked one 8-hour day, compressed into 12 minutes, against the real API and Postgres. Each person makes the same requests, in the same order, that the pages they use make. They spend work time in proportion to the size of the order, pick from the top of shared queues the way people do, and react to errors the way the screens let them: after a "changed by someone else" message they reload and try once more, and after a refusal or a crash they move on. At the end of the day an audit rebuilt every SKU's expected stock from the starting count, receipts and shipments, and compared it with the database.

Workload

  • 150 new sales orders plus 60 left over from yesterday.
  • 200 customers. The top 20 place 80% of orders and carry 120–300 customer prices each.
  • Order size: 10% have 20–40 lines of 100–900 units, 40% have 8–14 lines, and the rest are small, some just 1–2 lines of 3–4 units.
  • 500 SKUs with skewed popularity, stocked so that some orders go on backorder.
  • 12 vendors and 40 open vendor POs. Purchasing raises more during the day and the dock receives the ones that are due.

What the day looked like, run A

  • By mid-morning, pick & pack had packed and printed 74 orders, and every one stalled in Open Picks. Mark Shipped did nothing, because the 403 was never shown on screen.
  • Both dock clerks tried to receive every truck that was due and were refused 43 times.
  • Staff working from the tops of the same queues re-allocated or re-packed 23 orders that had already moved past that stage. This doubled reservations and put some orders back in Pick & Pack. Separately, two allocators promised the same 392 units of one SKU.
  • A key-account customer record passed 100 KB after the day's call notes, and every later save of that customer failed with a server error.

What the day looked like, run B

  • Shipping worked, but 9 shipments were confirmed a second time after a "changed by someone else" message. Each retry took the stock out again.
  • Three warehouse staff bulk-confirming overlapping Open Picks selections caused most of the damage. The list they worked from was already stale, so the stock was taken out for orders that someone else had already shipped.
  • At the end of the day, 15 SKUs showed negative on hand and 66 were off by 16,250 units in total. On paper, the physical shelves no longer match the system.

A throughput finding the fixes don't change: in all three runs, 74–85 orders were still waiting at validation at the end of the day. Checking and allocating an order are two separate decisions, and at about 210 orders a day two people cannot make both before closing. This depends on how long I assumed each review takes (about 1 minute plus 9–15 seconds per line for each decision). It's worth timing your own staff. If the numbers hold, you need a third person on that desk, or validation and allocation combined into one step for small orders.

05 · Changes

What was changed

API (server/)

  • New atomic endpoints POST /sales-orders/:so/ship, /undo-shipment and /vendor-purchase-orders/:po/receive. Each one locks the record, checks its version and status, and moves stock in the same transaction.
  • Allocation that adds to what an order holds is checked against free stock under an advisory lock (lib/inventory.ts).
  • Order writes need edit access on an order-workflow page. The optional expectedStatus field blocks stale screens, and a shipped order can't be changed back by a plain save.
  • Saving a whole order now clears fields the client left out.
  • qtyOnPurchaseOrder is recomputed inside PO transactions.
  • Permission checks match the pages that actually call each endpoint.
  • syncChildren writes only the rows that changed and refuses child ids that belong to another record.
  • ?open=1, ?shippedSince=, ?limit= and ?summary=1 list options.
  • Compare-and-set for Inventory Adjust, honest delete and constraint errors, a 5 MB request limit, login throttling, and sessions revoked on password reset.
  • Counter overrides can't be set to a number already in use. Saved reports now require the Reports permission.

Frontend (src/)

  • Stores return the server's saved copy, and pages keep it, so the next save doesn't conflict.
  • Validation, Allocation and Pick & Pack refuse orders that have already moved on, and show a notice saying so.
  • Allocation starts each line at the stock actually available.
  • Workflow pages and the Dashboard load open orders only.
  • Order Entry guards against double submits.
  • An expired session returns to the sign-in page.
  • Failed saves show their message instead of doing nothing.
  • The settings cache no longer keeps fallback values after a failed load.
  • Dates use local time, and the importer accepts M/D/YYYY and whole-number quantities only.
  • Receiving staff can receive from the PO page.
  • CSV exports are protected against formula injection and include a byte-order mark so Excel reads the encoding correctly.
  • The README and stale in-app help text are updated.

No schema migration was needed. The one behavior change to know about: allocation now refuses to reserve more than is free. The allocator gets a message naming each short SKU and how much is available. If you sometimes allocate ahead of a delivery on purpose, say so and I'll add an override for that.

06 · Next

Recommended next steps

  1. Change the admin password and set a password policy (at least 10 characters, and a forced change on first login). Anyone on the network can try admin / 123 today.
  2. Add paging to the remaining full-history pages: Shipment History, Closed Orders, Analytics, Reports and Item Profile. Also give Order Entry a customer list without price sheets, and load the chosen customer's prices on selection. These still download every order ever entered and will slow down again as the second year fills in.
  3. Serve the app and API from one origin over HTTPS. Passwords and session tokens currently cross the network unencrypted, and the frontend points at localhost:4000 unless it's rebuilt with a different address.
  4. Make returns and cancellations move stock. Receiving a return doesn't put units back on the shelf, and there is no way to cancel an order, so abandoned orders stay in the queues and keep holding stock.
  5. Store an item id on order and PO lines rather than the item number text, so renaming an item doesn't cut it off from its history.
  6. Add an audit trail for stock and orders covering who shipped, received, adjusted or allocated what, and when. The activity log currently covers accounts only.
  7. Add the Import fixes: per-row errors, a disabled button while saving, and skipping duplicates when an import is re-run.
  8. Run sim/day.mjs after significant changes. It finds this class of bug in 12 minutes.
07 · Reproduce

Running it yourself

cd server && npx prisma migrate deploy && npm run seed && npm run dev     # API on :4000
node sim/seed.mjs                                   # 15 staff, 500 SKUs, 200 customers, 40 POs
pg_dump -Fc erp_dev > seeded.dump                   # snapshot so every run starts equal
SIM_DUMP=seeded.dump SIM_CLIENT=fixed sim/run.sh presets 12     # one 12-minute day, audited
node sim/scale-seed.mjs 12                          # add 12 months of history (separate DB)
SIM_MODE=fixed node sim/scale-bench.mjs             # 15 simultaneous page loads
node sim/ui-check.mjs                               # browser check (needs Vite running)

Limits of this simulation: time was compressed 40×. Each person's work time comes from the assumptions stated above, not from measuring your staff. The data is synthetic. Everything ran on one machine, with the API, the database and the 15 simulated clients sharing its CPU. Absolute timings will differ on your server. The comparisons between runs and the stock and order audit results do not depend on the hardware.
